ABOUT ME

About me

I am a second-year Ph.D. student in Physics & Astronomy at the University of Delaware.

I completed my bachelor's degree in Physics at the Universidad Nacional de Colombia before moving to Delaware to begin my Ph.D., where I work with Prof. Sally Dodson-Robinson.

My research focuses on stellar variability and its impact on the detection and characterization of exoplanets. I am particularly interested in understanding the timescales over which stellar signals remain correlated.

Beyond research, I am strongly interested in science outreach, particularly in helping Colombian students learn about and access research opportunities in astronomy around the world.

STELLAR VARIABILITY IN PREPARATION

Measuring Persistence Time in Stellar Atmospheres

How long does stellar variability remember its past?

I use persistence time to quantify how quickly temporal correlations decay in stellar observations. My work adapts TAUEST to irregularly sampled astronomical time series and explores how observational cadence and temporal averaging affect the recovered timescale.

TAUEST

Persistence-time estimation for irregular time series.

TAUEST is a Python tool for estimating the characteristic decay time of correlated signals using an AR(1) model. It is designed to work with the uneven sampling commonly found in astronomical observations.
